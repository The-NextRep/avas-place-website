Course catalog

Twenty-one free mini-courses for life after the script.

Most courses run four short sessions and adapt to four learner tiers, from K–5 Sprouts to college Leaders. SAT Prep, Entrepreneurship and NIL & Recruiting are self-paced, six-module courses. Every course is free for families.

Knowing yourself

Confidence & Self-Worth

4 sessions · all four learner tiers

Confidence is built through reps, not born. Learners separate who they are from grades, likes and wins, and grow confidence through small, repeated acts of courage.

Core tool: The Evidence File. A running list of proof: things I tried, things I did while nervous, times I kept going.

  1. Session 1I Am More Than a ScoreWrite five "I am…" statements unrelated to grades, looks or wins.
  2. Session 2The Inner CriticRewrite harsh self-talk the way a coach would say it.
  3. Session 3Brave RepsPick one small brave act and do it this week.
  4. Session 4Speak UpGive a short talk on something you care about.
How it adapts by age
Sprouts
An "I Can" jar and strong-voice show-and-tell.
Builders
A real-vs.-edited social media check and a confidence playlist.
Launchers
An identity map separate from stats, GPA or followers.
Leaders
An imposter-feeling lab and a personal introduction for networking.

Real-World Rep: Complete three brave reps this week and add each one to your Evidence File.

Emotional Intelligence

4 sessions · all four learner tiers

Emotions are information. Build a bigger feelings vocabulary, read what others feel, and manage big emotions so you choose how to respond.

Core tool: The 3 Ns. Name it · Notice it · Navigate it.

  1. Session 1More Than Mad, Sad, GladBuild a feelings ladder from mild to intense.
  2. Session 2Read the RoomRead faces and body language, then check by asking.
  3. Session 3The Pause ButtonTry three reset moves and pick your go-to.
  4. Session 4Feelings in RelationshipsShare a feeling with "I feel… when… I need…"
How it adapts by age
Sprouts
Feelings-face cards and a feelings thermometer.
Builders
Reading tone in texts and a calm-down plan.
Launchers
Emotions under pressure and empathy in conflict.
Leaders
Emotional intelligence at work and in leadership.

Real-World Rep: Check in with the 3 Ns three times a day for a week.

Peer Pressure & Independent Choices

4 sessions · all four learner tiers

Fitting in shouldn't cost you who you are. Learners spot pressure in person and online, practice saying no without losing face, and make choices they can own.

Core tool: The STOP check. Spot the pressure · Think ahead · Options: name at least two · Pick and own it.

  1. Session 1Spot the PressureSort ten scenarios by type of pressure.
  2. Session 2The Cool NoPractice five ways to say no.
  3. Session 3Your CrewMap who makes good choices easier and who makes them harder.
  4. Session 4Stand on Your OwnRole-play a group scenario using STOP.
How it adapts by age
Sprouts
Puppet scenes and a strong "No thanks" voice.
Builders
Group-chat pressure, online trends and an exit line with a parent.
Launchers
Plan ahead for three real situations expected this year.
Leaders
A personal values list and one boundary to hold this semester.

Real-World Rep: Use STOP once this week when you feel pressure. Log what you chose and why.

My Voice, My Plan

4 sessions · all four learner tiers

You are the expert on you. Students with disabilities, and anyone who needs support, practice knowing their strengths and needs, speaking up for them, leading their own plans and knowing their rights at school, in college and at work.

Core tool: The Self-Advocacy Script. What I'm good at · What helps me · What I'm asking for · Why it helps me do my best.

  1. Session 1Know YourselfMake a one-page profile: strengths, challenges, what helps and what doesn't.
  2. Session 2Speak UpUse the Self-Advocacy Script to ask for an accommodation or support.
  3. Session 3Lead Your MeetingPlan and rehearse leading part of your own IEP, 504 or support meeting.
  4. Session 4Know Your Rights, Plan Your Next StepMap your next steps: college disability services, workplace accommodations and who to contact.
How it adapts by age
Sprouts
Naming what helps me learn, using a "help card," and asking a grown-up for a break or for help.
Builders
Understanding your own learning profile, joining part of your IEP or 504 meeting, and asking teachers for support.
Launchers
Leading your IEP meeting, transition planning, and practicing how to ask for accommodations in college and at work.
Leaders
Registering with college disability services, workplace accommodations, your rights under the ADA, and building a support team.

Real-World Rep: Use the Self-Advocacy Script once this week to ask for something that helps you.

Working with others

Bullying: Stand Strong, Stand Up

4 sessions · all four learner tiers

No one deserves to be bullied or has to face it alone. Tell bullying apart from conflict, know what to do if it happens to you, and step in safely for others.

Core tool: The 4 Upstander Moves. Notice it · Name it · Support the person targeted · Report it.

  1. Session 1Bullying or Conflict?Sort scenarios: conflict, a mean moment or bullying.
  2. Session 2If It's Happening to YouPractice a confident response and how to tell an adult.
  3. Session 3From Bystander to UpstanderRole-play the 4 Upstander Moves.
  4. Session 4Online and OfflineScreenshot, block, report and tell.
How it adapts by age
Sprouts
Puppet scenes, a strong voice and telling a grown-up.
Builders
Group chats, rumors and exclusion.
Launchers
Cyberbullying, hazing and supporting a friend.
Leaders
Hazing, harassment and reporting channels.

Real-World Rep: Make one upstander move this week and log it.

Open the course

Find Your People

4 sessions · all four learner tiers

Lots of young people feel lonely, even when they're always online, and making friends is a skill anyone can practice. Learners practice starting conversations, joining in, keeping friendships strong and repairing them when things go wrong.

Core tool: The Friendship Loop. Show up (go where people are) · Start small (a question or a compliment) · Follow up (remember something and come back to it) · Repair (own it when things go wrong).

  1. Session 1Where Are Your People?List three places to meet people who share an interest, and pick one to try.
  2. Session 2Conversation StartersPractice openers, follow-up questions and friendly ways to end a conversation.
  3. Session 3Keeping FriendsPlan one follow-up: a message, an invite, or remembering something they told you.
  4. Session 4Repairing a FriendshipPractice an apology that names what happened, owns it and asks how to make it right.
How it adapts by age
Sprouts
Joining a game at recess, taking turns, and what to do when you feel left out.
Builders
Friend groups that shift, group chats, and finding a club or team that fits you.
Launchers
Balancing screen time with in-person time, finding your people after a move, and healthy vs. draining friendships.
Leaders
Making friends in college or a new city, roommates and clubs, and loneliness in the first year.

Real-World Rep: Try one Friendship Loop step this week, in person if you can, and log what happened.

Conflict Resolution & Compromise

4 sessions · all four learner tiers

Disagreement is normal; how you handle it is a skill. Learners separate the problem from the person and look for the need underneath each position.

Core tool: PAUSE. Pause and breathe · Ask what happened from their side · Understand the need behind it · Share your side with an "I" statement · Explore options that meet both needs.

  1. Session 1Same Story, Two SidesRewrite a blame sentence as an "I" statement.
  2. Session 2Listen to UnderstandRepeat back what you heard before replying.
  3. Session 3Needs, Not PositionsFind the need behind three common arguments.
  4. Session 4Find the MiddleRole-play a conflict start to finish using PAUSE.
How it adapts by age
Sprouts
Puppet and picture-card role-plays about sharing and taking turns.
Builders
Group-chat and lunch-table scenarios, including online misunderstandings.
Launchers
Team projects, curfews and playing time; negotiate a written compromise.
Leaders
Draft a roommate agreement, plus a coworker scenario.

Real-World Rep: Use PAUSE in one real disagreement. Note which step was hardest.

Handling Uncertainty & Ambiguity

4 sessions · all four learner tiers

When there is no script, you can still take a first step. Learners practice breaking open-ended problems into what I know, what I don't, and what I can try.

Core tool: Know / Don't Know / Try card. Three boxes learners fill in whenever a task feels unclear.

  1. Session 1No Instructions IncludedFill the Know / Don't Know / Try card for a task with missing directions, then attempt it.
  2. Session 2Good Questions Beat GuessesTurn "I don't get it" into one specific question.
  3. Session 3Small BetsTry three quick versions of a solution and keep the one that works best.
  4. Session 4Comfortable With Not KnowingA group challenge whose rules change halfway through, then a debrief on how they adapted.
How it adapts by age
Sprouts
"Mystery Box" clue game and a picture-based Try card.
Builders
A deliberately vague group project brief; teams write their own success criteria.
Launchers
"Your manager quits mid-shift": map the options and pick a first move.
Leaders
Work a real ambiguous assignment, draft questions for the professor, submit an early draft.

Real-World Rep: Pick one unclear task this week. Fill the card before starting, then note what actually happened.

Absorbing & Applying Feedback

4 sessions · all four learner tiers

Feedback is about the work, not your worth. Learners practice hearing it, sorting it and using it.

Core tool: Hear it, Sort it, Use it. Hear it without defending. Sort each comment into use now, ask about or set aside. Use one piece right away.

  1. Session 1Work ≠ WorthPractice "The feedback is about ___, not about me."
  2. Session 2The Sting Is NormalReceive practice feedback and respond only with "Thanks, tell me more."
  3. Session 3Sort the PileSort mixed feedback into Use / Ask / Set Aside.
  4. Session 4Revise and ResubmitRevise your own work using one comment; share the before and after.
How it adapts by age
Sprouts
"Two Stars and a Wish" drawing critique.
Builders
Peer review with sentence starters.
Launchers
A revision plan for an essay or lab report, plus a mock interview debrief.
Leaders
Bring a real graded paper; draft a professional reply and a revision plan.

Real-World Rep: Ask someone for feedback on one thing you made this week. Use one comment and log the result.

Managing yourself

Time Management & Prioritizing

4 sessions · all four learner tiers

You can't do everything, so choose on purpose. Learners sort tasks by importance, protect focus time and plan around their real energy.

Core tool: Big Rocks & the Energy Ledger. Sort tasks into Must / Should / Could, and check school, social and physical energy before planning.

  1. Session 1Big Rocks FirstSort a week of real tasks into Must / Should / Could.
  2. Session 2Where Did the Time Go?Time-log one afternoon and compare guesses to reality.
  3. Session 3The Phone ProblemA phone-away focus sprint, then compare output.
  4. Session 4Plan BackwardBreak one assignment into dated steps on a calendar.
How it adapts by age
Sprouts
Picture schedules, first-then boards and sand-timer races.
Builders
Planner setup and a 10-minute focus sprint.
Launchers
Balance school, work, sports and family with the Energy Ledger.
Leaders
Map every syllabus deadline onto one semester calendar.

Real-World Rep: Run one week with a Big Rocks list each morning. Count how many Musts got done.

Resilience & Perseverance

4 sessions · all four learner tiers

A setback is data, not a verdict. Learners practice bouncing back so a bad grade or failed try becomes the next attempt, not the end.

Core tool: The Reset Rep. Feel it (name the emotion) · Face it (what actually happened?) · Fix one thing · Try again.

  1. Session 1Everyone MissesShare or privately write about one failure and what came after.
  2. Session 2Name It to Tame ItPractice a 60-second reset: breathe, name the feeling, one next step.
  3. Session 3Autopsy, Not AttackAnalyze a sample failed test: what went wrong and one fix.
  4. Session 4Attempt TwoA deliberately hard challenge, logging each attempt and what changed.
How it adapts by age
Sprouts
The "yet" power word and multi-try building games.
Builders
"Friction Reps" that get a little harder each round.
Launchers
A post-test recovery plan and a rejection role-play.
Leaders
A first-semester setback plan, including campus support resources.

Real-World Rep: Next time something goes wrong, run the Reset Rep and log your second attempt.

4 sessions · all four learner tiers

Knowing facts is step one; using them on a messy real problem is the goal. Learners practice defining problems, checking claims and testing solutions.

Core tool: IDEAL. Identify the problem · Define what solved looks like · Explore options and evidence · Act on the best one · Look back and learn.

  1. Session 1What's the Real Problem?Ask "why?" five times on a real problem.
  2. Session 2Check the ClaimWho says it, what's the evidence, what's missing.
  3. Session 3Use What You KnowSolve a real-world problem with a school concept.
  4. Session 4Solve It TogetherTeams work through IDEAL and pitch a solution in two minutes.
How it adapts by age
Sprouts
Detective mysteries and fix-it challenges.
Builders
Spot the misleading post; plan a fundraiser with a budget.
Launchers
A community problem project with a proposal to a real decision-maker.
Leaders
Case-study teams and a written recommendation memo.

Real-World Rep: Pick one real problem you face. Work it through IDEAL on paper and try your solution.

College & career readiness

Hired: First Job Ready

4 sessions · all four learner tiers

Landing and keeping a first job takes skills most schools don't teach. Learners build a first resume, practice interviewing, and learn the workplace habits employers say new hires are missing.

Core tool: The Hired Habits. Show up on time · Communicate clearly by text, email and in person · Own your mistakes · Ask for feedback.

  1. Session 1Your First ResumeBuild a one-page resume from school, sports, volunteering and side jobs.
  2. Session 2Applying and InterviewingPractice answers to five common questions, then do a mock interview with feedback.
  3. Session 3Workplace CommunicationRewrite messages to a manager: calling out sick, asking for time off, asking a question.
  4. Session 4Keeping the JobPractice owning a mistake, asking for feedback, and asking for more hours.
How it adapts by age
Sprouts
Classroom jobs, being on time, and being someone others can count on.
Builders
First ways to earn (babysitting, pet sitting, yard work), a simple flyer, and being reliable.
Launchers
A first part-time job, work permits and hour rules, a resume and your first interview.
Leaders
Internships, a professional online profile, professional email, and your first full-time role.

Real-World Rep: Apply for one real job, internship or volunteer role, or do a full mock interview with an adult.

Build Your Future: Trades & Careers

4 sessions · all four learner tiers

College isn't the only road. Learners explore skilled trades and other career paths, from electrician, HVAC, plumbing and welding to manufacturing and more, and compare apprenticeships, certificates and college side by side.

Core tool: The Path Compare. For any path: what you'd do all day · how long the training takes and what it costs · how you can earn while you learn · what it pays and where it can lead.

  1. Session 1Hands-On CareersExplore three trades: what each does, the training and the job outlook, using the Occupational Outlook Handbook.
  2. Session 2Earn While You LearnCompare an apprenticeship, a certificate and a degree with the Path Compare.
  3. Session 3Try It OutDo a hands-on challenge, like wiring a simple circuit or measuring and building, or set up a job shadow.
  4. Session 4Your Path PlanWrite a plan: classes, programs or apprenticeships to look into, and one person to talk to.
How it adapts by age
Sprouts
Community helpers who build and fix things, and simple building challenges.
Builders
A career interest quiz, tool and safety basics, and career and technical classes to look for in high school.
Launchers
Apprenticeships, career and technical education, dual enrollment and industry certifications.
Leaders
Changing paths, short certificate programs, union and employer apprenticeships, and starting a trade business.

Real-World Rep: Talk to one person who works in a trade or career you're curious about, and write down three things you learned.

SAT Prep

Grades 10–12 · 6 self-paced modules, about 60–90 minutes each

The SAT rewards calm pacing and fixing repeat mistakes more than cramming. This course applies the skills from Courses 1–7 to one high-stakes test on the road to college.

Core tool: The Error Log. For every missed question: topic · why I missed it · the fix · retest date.

  1. Module 1Know the TestA timed diagnostic module in the Bluebook app; start the Error Log.
  2. Module 2Reading & Writing StrategyTimed practice; log misses by type.
  3. Module 3Math Strategy + DesmosTimed practice using the built-in calculator where it saves time.
  4. Module 4Pacing Per ModuleFlag, skip and return across two timed modules.
  5. Module 5Test-Day NervesA full practice test under real conditions, with the Reset Rep.
  6. Module 6Personal Study PlanBuild a 6–8 week plan from your top three mistake types.

Uses the free official practice tests in Bluebook and Official Digital SAT Prep on Khan Academy. Families pay nothing extra.

Real-World Rep: Pick a target test date and complete two weeks of your study plan.

Entrepreneurship

College & first-year adults · 6 self-paced modules

Take a small idea from problem to first paying customer in six weeks, using uncertainty, feedback, resilience and problem-solving as working tools.

Core tool: The Build–Test–Learn Board. One page per week: what we believe · what we tested · what customers said or did · what we'll change. A mentor reviews it.

  1. Module 1Problems Worth SolvingList ten problems you or classmates face; pick one.
  2. Module 2Talk to CustomersInterview five potential customers. No pitching.
  3. Module 3The Smallest TestDesign a test for under $50.
  4. Module 4Money BasicsPrice, cost, margin and break-even.
  5. Module 5Pivot or PersistKeep, change or drop the idea based on what you learned.
  6. Module 6Pitch DayRecord a 90-second pitch; a panel of founders sends written feedback.

Pick a campus service, a digital product, student-made goods or a social-impact venture.

Real-World Rep: Make one real sale, sign-up or pre-order, and log what it took.

Online Safety & Scam Smarts

4 sessions · all four learner tiers

Scammers count on panic and shame. Learners practice slowing down, spotting red flags and knowing exactly what to do and who to tell.

Core tool: The 3 Rs. Recognize the red flags · Refuse to send, pay or click · Report it to a trusted adult and the platform.

  1. Session 1Too Good to Be TrueSort realistic messages into safe, suspicious or scam.
  2. Session 2Who's Really There?Check a profile for signs it's fake.
  3. Session 3Never Pay, Never PanicRole-play the first ten minutes after a threat.
  4. Session 4Your Safety TeamBuild a no-shame family safety plan.
How it adapts by age
Sprouts
"Tricky people online" picture cards and always telling a grown-up.
Builders
Game-chat scams, fake giveaways and privacy settings.
Launchers
Sextortion warning signs, fake jobs and how to report.
Leaders
Rental, job and payment-app scams; protecting your identity.

Real-World Rep: Do a privacy checkup on two apps and share your safety plan with a trusted adult.

Smart About AI

4 sessions · all four learner tiers

AI is in homework, games and phones. Learners find out what AI is and isn't, catch it when it's wrong, protect their privacy and use it honestly, with families learning alongside.

Core tool: The AI Check. Who made it and what is it for? · Is it right? Check it against a trusted source · Is it safe to share? Never personal information · Is it honest to use here? Follow your teacher's rules and say when you used it.

  1. Session 1What Is AI, Really?Sort everyday tools into AI or not, and explain in your own words how AI learns from examples.
  2. Session 2When AI Gets It WrongFact-check three AI answers against a trusted source and spot made-up details.
  3. Session 3Private Means PrivateMake a "never share" list and check the privacy settings on one app with a parent.
  4. Session 4Using AI HonestlyWrite your own AI-use rules for homework and practice telling a teacher how you used AI.
How it adapts by age
Sprouts
Robot-or-person guessing games, "AI makes mistakes too," and never typing your name or address. Best done with a parent or teacher.
Builders
AI in games and apps, fact-checking, fake images and videos, and your school's AI rules.
Launchers
Using AI to study vs. to cheat, spotting AI-made misinformation, and how AI is changing jobs.
Leaders
AI at college and work, citing AI use, privacy and data, and writing your own AI-use rules.

Real-World Rep: Do an AI Check on one AI answer with a family member and talk about what you found.

NIL & Recruiting

Grades 9–12 & college athletes, with a parent track · 6 self-paced modules

Your name, image and likeness can open doors, or close them if you sign the wrong thing. Learn the rules, the money and the red flags before the first offer.

Core tool: The Deal Check. Is it allowed? · What do I give and get? · How am I paid and what will I owe in taxes? · Who reviewed it?

  1. Module 1Know Your RulesFind your state's NIL policy and the rules that apply to you.
  2. Module 2Your Brand, Your StoryAudit your social media and write a brand statement.
  3. Module 3Reading the DealMark up a sample NIL agreement.
  4. Module 4Money & TaxesSet aside for taxes and start an income tracker.
  5. Module 5Recruiting & NIL TogetherBuild a recruiting plan that fits your NIL goals.
  6. Module 6Pitch DayRecord a pitch to a local business.

Education, not legal or tax advice. Rules differ by state, so check with your state athletic association or compliance office.

Real-World Rep: Run one real or sample offer through the Deal Check with a parent or mentor.
